# 0124 — Rain: drops around the camera, wet materials, puddles with ripples, shelter from above

Status: **KEEP** (a new node; without it nothing of it runs) · Branch `fellow/geometry` · Node `FellowRain3D` (module
`fellow_fluid`) · Code: `modules/fellow_fluid/fellow_rain_3d.{h,cpp}` (the node, its drops, splashes and heightfield,
the ripple texture on the render thread), `fellow_rain.glsl` (ripples, mip levels);
`servers/rendering/renderer_rd/effects/fellow_weather.{h,cpp}` (the renderer's weather state: a uniform buffer and two
textures), the wetting in `shaders/forward_clustered/scene_forward_clustered.glsl` (`fellow_wetness_apply`, the
specialization `fellow_wetness`), bindings 49-51 of the scene's uniform set 1 in `render_forward_clustered.cpp`; the
ripples on `FellowWater3D` (`fellow_water_shader.h`); bench scene Rain Lab (`--scene=rain`).

## Problem

The user asked (2026-10-29), after water (0123), for the look of rain and wet surfaces. Stock Godot has no rain: a
project makes the drops with particles of its own and wets nothing (a wet material is a second material per surface,
swapped by hand), and nothing keeps the ground under a roof dry.

## Change

`FellowRain3D` is the rain; one is active at a time (the last to enter the tree).

**Drops** are stock GPU particles (an internal `GPUParticles3D`): `max_drops` (24 000) in a box of `area` (40 m)
around the camera, starting `fall_height` (14 m) above it and upwind by the fall's drift, falling at `drop_speed`
(9 m/s) plus `wind`. Their material is a spatial shader of its own: a streak of `drop_size` (2.4 x 55 cm, the path a
frame's exposure sees) along the particle's velocity (aligned with it: the wind's lean; a fixed-Y billboard stood them
upright) turned to face the camera, soft across and thinning to both ends; **lit** — the sky's ambient and the scene's
lights, brighter against a light behind the drop (water scatters forward) — so the rain at dusk is dark but where the
lamps shine through it; not drawn within 0.5 m of the camera and faded in to 4 m (a drop at the lens would be a
blurred bar across the screen). `rain` scales their number (`amount_ratio`). They stop on the scene as seen from
above (collision with the heightfield below, hide on contact) and spawn two splash droplets there (a sub-emitter at
collision; `splashes`), camera-facing, fading over their 0.3 s. A fixed seed: the same drops every run.

**Shelter.** A `GPUParticlesCollisionHeightField3D` (internal; `occlusion_size` 80 m, `occlusion_height` 80 m,
`occlusion_resolution` 1024: 8 cm a texel) follows the camera: the scene's top seen from above. It is the drops'
collider and it tells the renderer where the rain reaches: its texture (the particles storage's heightfield, now
exposed with `fellow_particles_collision_get_heightfield_texture`) is bound to the scene shader, decoded as the
particles shader does (normalized height h → top = origin.y + (2h − 1) half height). A surface more than 50 cm
below the top is dry, one within 25 cm of it wet (one bilinear lookup). A wall samples just outside itself along its
normal: the roof above its own footprint would keep it dry. Beyond the heightfield everything is exposed. It is
rendered again when it moves with the camera or every frame (`occlusion_always_update`, for moving roofs). It moves
by sixteenths of its size (5 m; stock's follow-camera mode moves it by 1 m): whole texels, so the shelter's edges stay
put between renders, and a fifth of the renders. A drop is stopped up to a step below the surface it hits (hide on
contact leaves it there) and its splash starts there: the splashes collide with the heightfield too, which puts them
back on the surface (a roof's splashes showed below its ceiling).

**Wet materials** (Forward+). The renderer keeps a weather state (`RendererRD::FellowWeather`: wetness, puddles, rain,
time, the heightfield's placement, the ripple texture), uploaded as a uniform buffer while a rain is active. With it,
the scene shader's specialization `fellow_wetness` (in the depth pre-pass's normal-roughness variant and the colour
pass; never the alpha pass) runs `fellow_wetness_apply` after the material's own `fragment()`, on its albedo,
roughness, metallic and normal, for every opaque material (a `ShaderMaterial` too):

* the surface's wetness: `wetness` × exposed, undersides only 10 %;
* porous materials (rough, not metallic) darken to half as water fills their pores; every wet surface gets a glossy
  film (roughness towards ≤ 0.2);
* **puddles** on flat ground (geometric normal above 0.95 up), rough and not metallic: a value noise at three scales
  (cells of 4.3 m, 1.1 m and 27 cm, the finer two turned) thresholded by `puddles`, appearing above wetness 0.4:
  flat, smooth (roughness 0.03), standing water, the ripples' slopes as its normal;
* **ripples**: a 256² tiling texture of slopes (`ripple_tile`, 1.5 m) computed every frame on the GPU
  (`fellow_rain.glsl`: three layers of 5 x 5 cells, each cell's own period bringing a drop with probability `rain` at
  a hashed point; its ring spreads at 0.25 m/s and fades within 0.55 s), sampled twice (offset, scaled 0.77: no
  visible repeat); on wet flat ground about a third of them as the film's. Its alpha holds the puddles' noise (a
  tiling value noise of 16 x 16 cells): three lookups instead of three octaves of hashed noise per pixel. Two
  dispatches build it with its mip levels: one 16 x 16 tile per group writes level 0 and reduces itself in shared
  memory to levels 1-4, one group the rest. The lookups take an explicit mip level computed from the position's
derivatives before the early returns.

A sheltered surface returns at once, a wall or slope after the darkening and the film (no noise, no ripples), and the
ripples are not sampled while no rain falls.

In the pre-pass too: screen-space reflections and SSAO read the wet normal and roughness, so the puddles
reflect the street in SSR. Without an active rain the specialization is off: the shader variants are stock's.
`rendering/fellow/fluid/enabled` off turns the wetting off.

`wetness_follows_rain` makes the wetness follow the rain (wet in `wetting_time` at full rain, dry in
`drying_time`). A **FellowWater3D** adds the ripples' slopes to its surface's normal while the rain falls.

**Water's reflection march** (with the rain, the Water Lab's): the clip position along the ray is linear in t and
the view depth comes from two rows of the inverse projection (no matrix per step); the same image to float rounding.

## Results

**Exactness.** Without a `FellowRain3D` nothing of it runs (the specialization off; bindings 49-51 default): City
Lab and Rain Lab `--rain=off` bit for bit the images of the build before (`5e26af0`); Water Lab within float rounding
(the march's arithmetic); Fluid Lab within its own run-to-run noise (1-3 pixels by 1 level, as between two runs of
the same build).

**Look** (Rain Lab, lavapipe 960 x 540). A town street (asphalt, sidewalks, plaster and brick, a cobbled square with
a fountain basin, an arcade, a bus shelter, a parked car, benches, trees, street lamps). Raining: the walls darker,
the asphalt and paving glossy, puddles in the street and the square reflecting the buildings, poles and lamps (SSR),
rings of ripples on them and on the basin; the car's paint not darkened (metal); the arcade's floor and the bus
shelter's ground dry; streaks of rain over everything, splashes at the ground. At dusk the street lamps' reflections
stretch over the wet asphalt and break up in the puddles' ripples.

**The ripples' cells shared** (milestone 26, exact): each texel searched its 3 x 3 neighbours in three layers — 27
cells, each one's drop worked out from four to six hashes — though the tile has only 75 cells. Each group of the
ripples' dispatch now works them out once into shared memory (the same expressions: the same values), with the
layers' offsets; the cells wrap by one tile without an integer modulo, the noise's lattice with a mask. Rain Lab
without drops (`--rain_drops=0`: the particles are not deterministic from run to run) bit for bit; the ripples'
compute on lavapipe 14.1-15.6 → 9.4-10.0 ms (−33 to −36 %), on the RTX a few hundredths of a millisecond.

## Decision

**KEEP** — a new node, nothing changes without it. Raining costs 0.36 ms of a 1080p frame on the RTX 5070 Ti Laptop,
a third of it the screen-space reflections the wet surfaces now get.
